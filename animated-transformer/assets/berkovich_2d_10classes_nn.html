Detailed Structure of Upward and Downward Movements at a (II, II) Divisorial Vertex

2-Dimensional generalization of parent ($\infty$) and child ($\gamma \in \mathbb{F}_p$) branching directions over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

1. 1D Berkovich Space ($\mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$) at Type II Vertex:
  • Total Directions: $|\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)| = p + 1$ directions.
  • 1 Upward Edge ($\gamma = \infty$): Moves up to larger radius $\rho + 1$ (parent disk).
  • $p$ Downward Edges ($\gamma \in \mathbb{F}_p$): Move down to smaller radius $\rho - 1$ into $p$ child disks.
2. 2D Berkovich Space ($\mathbb{A}^2_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$) at (II, II) Vertex:

The tangent space of directions is $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_p)$, containing $p^2 + p + 1$ total faces. $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ partitions into:

$$\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_p) = \underbrace{\mathbb{F}_p \times \mathbb{F}_p}_{p^2 \text{ Downward Faces}} \;\cup\; \underbrace{\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)}_{p+1 \text{ Upward/Boundary Faces}}$$
3. Upward vs Downward Face Breakdown:
$p^2$ Downward Faces ($\mathbb{F}_p \times \mathbb{F}_p$): Refining precision in both variables $X$ and $Y$ simultaneously, moving down to smaller log-radii $(\rho_1 - 1, \rho_2 - 1)$ into one of $p \times p = p^2$ child poly-disks (sub-boxes).
$p + 1$ Upward / Boundary Faces ($\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$):
  • 1 Purely Upward Face $(\infty, \infty)$: Moving up to larger log-radii $(\rho_1 + 1, \rho_2 + 1)$ toward master parent poly-disk.
  • $p$ Mixed Boundary Faces: Expanding radius in one variable while refining a child branch in the other.

Example for $p = 3$: Total faces $= 3^2 + 3 + 1 = \mathbf{13 \text{ faces}}$. There are $3^2 = \mathbf{9 \text{ downward faces}}$ (refining child sub-boxes) and $3 + 1 = \mathbf{4 \text{ upward faces}}$ (expanding up to parent boxes).

The Intuitive Analogy: Shadows on the Wall vs. 3D Reality

Imagine a 3D object and its 2D shadows on the X and Y walls. If the object is a Cube ($P_{\text{prod}}$), its shadows on both walls are squares, and combining them recreates the cube. But if the object is a Diagonal Pipe ($P_{\text{tube}}$), its 1D projections look identical to the cube's shadows, yet the variables $X$ and $Y$ are deeply entangled! In Berkovich spaces, a 2D point is not always just two 1D points pasted together.
